# Efficient nonlinear flame response modeling for propulsion thermoacoustic analysis using limited numerical data — Dual-Path network (2024)

- **Authors / venue:** J. Wu, T. Wang, J. Nan, W. Han, L. Yang, J. Li (Beihang University). Acta Astronautica (the arXiv v1 title was "A Dual-Path neural network model to construct the flame nonlinear thermoacoustic response in the time domain").
- **Links:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.05885 · code https://github.com/wujiaweii/Constructing-the-flame-nonlinear-response · data on Zenodo (record linked in the paper)
- **Source read:** tex (arXiv e-print)
- **Thread:** 4 combustion

## Summary
Learns the **nonlinear flame response** of a 2D axisymmetric laminar premixed flame (OpenFOAM reactingFoam) as a time-domain map from the inlet velocity-perturbation history to the global heat-release-rate fluctuation: `q'(t) = f(u'(t-(n-1)dt), …, u'(t))`, with n=6000 samples of memory, sparsely resampled to 1000. The **training data are linear frequency sweeps (10→1000 Hz) at 10 amplitudes (A=0.1…1.0)**. Testing is on 72 single-frequency harmonic signals (100–900 Hz, A 0.25–0.95), giving an average MRE of 6.69%. The model is a dual path: CNN-embedding + 4 LSTMs (chronological path) and a transformer-style temporal-detail path, coupled by cross-attention "injector" modules. Baselines: an MLP (Tathawadekar et al. 2021) and an LSTM (Yadav et al. 2022), matched at ~1M parameters.

## Relevance to FlameBench
**The closest problem setup in the whole list — almost our dataset design:** a forced laminar flame, **frequency-sweep forcing at multiple amplitudes for training, harmonic signals for testing** (our data: `sineSweep_f1_f80_A02/A04`). Implications:
1. **Adopt their train/test split philosophy:** train on sweeps, test on single-frequency harmonics (and unseen amplitudes). This is a clean, physically meaningful generalisation test for FlameBench.
2. **Add a global metric:** integrated heat-release response q'(t) and the derived **FTF/FDF (gain and phase vs frequency and amplitude)** from our field predictions. This is what the combustion community cares about.
3. Their scalar models (MLP, LSTM, Dual-Path) are **"0-D" baselines**: if a full-field surrogate can't beat a direct phi → q' model on the global response, that's important to know.
4. Follow-up references cited there, worth fetching: Tathawadekar et al. 2021 (MLP flame response, PROCI); Yadav et al. 2022 (physics-informed LSTM flame response).
