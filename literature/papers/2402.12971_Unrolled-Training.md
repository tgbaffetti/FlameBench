# Differentiability in Unrolled Training of Neural Physics Simulators on Transient Dynamics (2024)

- **Authors / venue:** B. List, L.-W. Chen, K. Bali, N. Thuerey (TU Munich). arXiv 2024 (v2 Oct 2024).
- **Links:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12971 · code https://github.com/tum-pbs/unrolling
- **Source read:** tex (arXiv e-print)
- **Thread:** 6 rollout stability

## Summary
A large empirical study (400+ models) that separates the two effects of unrolled training: (a) **fixing the train/test distribution shift** (the model sees its own outputs) and (b) **long-term gradients** through the rollout. It compares one-step, fully differentiable unrolling ("WIG") and **unrolling without temporal gradients ("NOG")**, in both pure prediction and solver-correction setups, on KS, wake flow, Kolmogorov turbulence, 3D KS and an aero case. Main findings: unrolling helps almost always. Differentiable unrolling is best. **No-gradient unrolling gets close** at a fraction of the cost (it is essentially pushforward extended to many steps). The paper also gives practical guidance on choosing the unroll horizon.

## Relevance to FlameBench
Provides the justification and the **default settings for a unified stabilisation protocol**. Recommendation: implement `unroll_steps` and `unroll_grad ∈ {full, none}` in `train.py`. `unroll_steps=2, grad=none` reproduces the MP-PDE pushforward; `unroll_steps=m, grad=full` is classic unrolling, as used in the coastal-KAE and ACDM papers. Note: a solver-in-the-loop "correction" setup is not applicable for us (no coarse flame solver in the loop), so only the prediction-setup conclusions apply.
