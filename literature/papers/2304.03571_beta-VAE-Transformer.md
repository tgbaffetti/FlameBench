# β-Variational autoencoders and transformers for reduced-order modelling of fluid flows (2023)

- **Authors / venue:** A. Solera-Rico, C. Sanmiguel Vila, M. Á. Gómez, Y. Wang, A. Almashjary, S. T. M. Dawson, R. Vinuesa (UC3M, INTA, KTH and others). Nature Communications 15, 1361 (2024).

## Summary
A convolutional **β-VAE** compresses 2D velocity fluctuation snapshots (300x98 grid, u and v channels, standardised per pixel) to a small, **near-orthogonal, disentangled latent** whose modes resemble POD modes but are more compact. A **transformer** (time-space embedding, pooling, self-attention or "easy attention") predicts the next latent vector from a time-delay window. Compared with LSTM and a Koopman-with-forcing (KNF) model, and assessed with Poincaré maps.

## Benchmark fields
| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Input geometry | Regular grid (CNN encoder); flow downsampled from nested IBPM grids |
| Exogenous conditioning | None (autonomous chaotic flow) |
| Latent reduction | **β-VAE** (disentangled, ranked modes) |
| Prediction target | Next latent vector from a time-delay window |
| Rollout & stability | AR in latent space; evaluated statistically (Poincaré maps, PDFs) rather than pointwise, because the flow is chaotic |
| Demonstrated scale | 300x98 x 2 channels; ~30k snapshots |
| Datasets / physics | 2D flow over two collinear flat plates, periodic and chaotic regimes |

## Relevance to FlameBench
Direct precedent for our `ae-transformer`. Two actionable ideas: (1) add a **β-KL term** to our AE to get ranked, interpretable, POD-comparable modes — a nice figure for the paper (AE modes vs POD modes of the flame); (2) evaluate with **statistical/attractor metrics** (PSD of probe signals, PDFs), not only MSE. That matters for a forced flame, where phase drift makes pointwise errors misleading.
