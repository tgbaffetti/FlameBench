# Fourier Neural Operator for Parametric Partial Differential Equations — FNO (2020)

- **Authors / venue:** Z. Li, N. Kovachki, K. Azizzadenesheli, B. Liu, K. Bhattacharya, A. Stuart, A. Anandkumar (Caltech). ICLR 2021.
- **Links:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08895 · code https://github.com/neuraloperator/neuraloperator (the original repo is zongyi-li/fourier_neural_operator)
- **Source read:** tex (arXiv e-print)
- **Thread:** 2 neural operators

## Summary
Neural-operator layers `v <- sigma(W v + F^-1(R · F(v)))`: a learned linear filter on a truncated set of Fourier modes plus a pointwise linear term. The paper gives two variants for time-dependent NS. **FNO-2D** maps the previous 10 steps to the next step with spatial 2D FFT and recurrent rollout. **FNO-3D** does space-time FFT and maps the initial steps to the whole trajectory.

## Benchmark fields
| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Input geometry | **Regular grid only (FFT)** |
| Exogenous conditioning | None in the paper. Our implementation broadcasts phi as 2 extra constant channels; alternatives are FiLM / AdaIN (GINO) or "spatial-spectral" conditioning (Gupta & Brandstetter, used in UPT's baselines) |
| Latent reduction | Spectral truncation (modes) |
| Prediction target | Absolute next state (FNO-2D) or the whole trajectory (FNO-3D) |
| Rollout & stability | FNO-2D: AR recurrent. FNO-3D: one-shot. No stability tricks |
| Demonstrated scale | 64x64 (up to 256x256 zero-shot), 1k-10k trajectories. FNO-3D wins with lots of data; **FNO-2D wins in low-data regimes** (N=1000 at nu=1e-4/1e-5) |
| Datasets / physics | Burgers, Darcy, 2D NS on the torus |

## Relevance to FlameBench
Our `FNO3DModel` is the paper's FNO-3D with the **n_past history as the third FFT axis**, keeping only the last output slice. Two observations:
1. An FFT along a 10-sample history axis is questionable (non-periodic, short). The paper's own **FNO-2D + channels-as-history** is the more natural next-step variant, and it did better in low-data settings like ours (2 trajectories).
2. It still needs a grid: use GINO (learned mesh↔grid) or a fixed interpolation; FFNO (factorized FNO) was the best model on regular grids in REALM.
Keep FNO as the **canonical baseline**, but reconsider the 3D-over-history design.
