# Modeling of the nonlinear flame response of a Bunsen-type flame via multi-layer perceptron (2021)

- **Authors / venue:** N. Tathawadekar, N. A. K. Doan, C. F. Silva, N. Thuerey (TU Munich). Proceedings of the Combustion Institute 38 (2021) 6261–6269 (open access, CC BY).
- **Links:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05234 · data on Zenodo (record 6451381)
- **Source read:** pdf (arXiv provides the published PDF only; text extracted with pdftotext)
- **Thread:** 4 combustion

## Summary
Learns the **nonlinear flame response** of the Kornilov laminar multi-slit premixed flame from **one unsteady CFD simulation** (122,300-cell structured grid), excited with a **broadband, low-pass-filtered velocity signal with uniformly distributed amplitudes**. An MLP (with dropout, hyperparameter sweep) maps the upstream velocity time series to heat-release-rate fluctuations. The trained model is then used to (i) compute the **flame describing function (FDF)** with mono-frequency excitations, capturing higher harmonics as well, and (ii) be **coupled with an acoustic solver** to predict thermoacoustic limit cycles. RMS amplitudes and dominant frequencies match CFD.

## Relevance to FlameBench
The **founding reference for "one forced CFD run → learned flame response"**. Together with the Dual-Path paper (2409.05885), it defines the combustion community's evaluation of forced-flame surrogates: **FDF gain/phase vs frequency and amplitude**, harmonic content, and limit-cycle prediction when coupled with acoustics. FlameBench's field-level models should be evaluated on these same integrated metrics, with this MLP as the **0-D baseline**. The same group's 2024 follow-up extends this to turbulent flames (Tathawadekar et al., Int. J. Spray Combust. Dyn., 2024 — not on arXiv; listed in NEEDS_MANUAL).
