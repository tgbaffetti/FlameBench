# Incomplete to complete multiphysics forecasting — a hybrid approach for learning unknown phenomena (2021)

- **Authors / venue:** N. Tathawadekar, N. A. K. Doan, C. F. Silva, N. Thuerey (TU Munich). arXiv 2021 (v2 2023); the tex uses a JFM-style template — journal version not verified.
- **Links:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.11185 · code+data https://github.com/tum-pbs/Hybrid-Solver-for-Reactive-Flows
- **Source read:** tex (arXiv e-print)
- **Thread:** 4 combustion / 6 rollout (unrolled training)

## Summary
Forecasts **2D laminar reacting flows (planar and Bunsen-type flames)**. A **differentiable incomplete PDE solver** (PhiFlow: non-reacting NS + transport, without the chemistry source terms) advances the flow one step, and a CNN (UNet) corrects T, Y_f and Y_o to recover the reacting state. It is trained **in the loop with m-step unrolling** through the solver. Compared with purely data-driven (PDD) ResNet/UNet and **FNO** baselines that map [T, Y_f, Y_o]_t → t+1. The hybrid is far more stable over long rollouts and generalises to non-uniform inflow. It also demonstrates **flame-shape control** by back-propagating through the hybrid solver.

## Relevance to FlameBench
One of very few **field-level ML forecasting papers on laminar flames** — cite as the closest *field* precedent. Its PDD baselines (ResNet/UNet/FNO predicting T and species next-step) failed on long rollouts, while physics-in-the-loop succeeded. That motivates reporting long-horizon metrics and stabilisation in FlameBench. The hybrid-solver track itself is out of scope unless a cheap non-reacting solver of our geometry is available.
